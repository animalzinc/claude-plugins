# Pinecrest Outfitters cut its weekly report from a day to an hour

Every Monday, the two-person data team at Pinecrest Outfitters spent the whole day building one report. Since March they spend an hour on it, and leadership reads it by 11 a.m. instead of Tuesday morning.

The report covers 38 stores and about 4,000 products. Its numbers didn’t change; the work behind them did.

## Monday used to disappear

Priya Raman, who leads data at Pinecrest, described the old routine as “copying the same six exports into the same spreadsheet and hoping nothing moved.” The exports came from the point-of-sale system, the warehouse and the web store, each on its own schedule.

## One model for three sources

The team rebuilt the report as a single AcmeBoard dashboard on top of one PostgreSQL model. Each source loads overnight, so the dashboard is ready before the first person logs in at 8 a.m.

On a busy weekend the stores sell about $1.2 million in gear, and the report is how leadership decides what to restock. Raman’s team also spent $18,000 less on contract analysts in the first half of the year.

## A different Monday

The hour that’s left goes to checking the numbers instead of assembling them. “We find the odd things now,” Raman said. “Last month it was a store selling 40 percent more tents than the one next to it. That turned out to be a pricing error.”
