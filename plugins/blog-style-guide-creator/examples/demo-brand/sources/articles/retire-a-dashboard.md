# How to retire a dashboard without losing anyone

By the end of this guide you’ll have a retirement routine that takes about an hour a month and tells every affected person before anything disappears. It works in AcmeBoard or in any tool that records when a dashboard was last opened.

## Before you start

- **Access.** Admin rights to the workspace, or a teammate who has them.
- **A list.** Every dashboard with its owner and the date it was last opened. In AcmeBoard this is the Usage tab; export it as a CSV.
- **A channel.** The place your company already announces data changes.

## Steps

1. Filter the list to dashboards nobody has opened in 90 days.
2. Message each owner with the dashboard’s name, its last-opened date and a deadline two weeks out.
3. Archive the dashboards whose owners agree or don’t reply by the deadline.
4. Post the archived list in your channel at 9 a.m. on a Monday, when people are most likely to read it.
5. Restore any dashboard someone asks for within 30 days and give it an owner as you do.

## Archive instead of deleting

Archiving keeps the queries and the history. If a quarterly report turns out to depend on a dashboard you retired, restoring it takes a minute; rebuilding it from memory takes a week.

## Keep the routine small

The first pass is the slow one. After that, a monthly run usually turns up two or three dashboards—the message to their owners takes a few minutes to send.
