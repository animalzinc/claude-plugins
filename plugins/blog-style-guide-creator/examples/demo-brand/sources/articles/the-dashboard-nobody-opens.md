# The dashboard nobody opens

Every data team has one. It took a week to build, it answered a question somebody asked in a quarterly review and it hasn’t been opened since March. Nobody deletes it—deleting feels like admitting the week was wasted.

In AcmeBoard usage data from the workspaces that agreed to share it last year, 61 percent of the dashboards created in a given quarter were opened fewer than five times after their first month. The median workspace in that data had 140 dashboards and nine people who looked at any of them.

## Old dashboards cost more than storage

A stale dashboard isn’t free. It shows up in search, so a new analyst finds three versions of “weekly revenue” and has to ask which one is real. It runs its queries on a schedule, so the warehouse bill carries it. It also teaches the company that the data team’s work expires.

## Ownership fixes most of it

The workspaces with the fewest stale dashboards had one habit in common: every dashboard had one named person as its owner. When that person changed roles, the dashboard found a new owner or went to the archive—nobody kept it out of politeness.

Ownership doesn’t need a process document. It needs three things on each dashboard:

- **Owner.** The person who answers questions about it.
- **Question.** The decision it exists to inform, in one sentence.
- **Review date.** The day someone checks whether that decision still gets made.

## Start with the last quarter

You don’t need to audit 140 dashboards at once. Sort by last opened, take everything untouched for 90 days and send each owner a short note. Most will say archive it. The ones that come back with a reason are the dashboards worth keeping—and they now have an owner.
